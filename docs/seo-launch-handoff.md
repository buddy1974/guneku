## A. Current indexability state

All of this is measured against production, not inferred from the source.

### Robots

`/robots.txt` is generated by `src/app/robots.ts`. One rule group, `User-Agent: *`,
`Allow: /`, with these disallowed:

```
/api/   /sign-in   /sign-up   /indigenes/profile
/indigenes/onboarding   /indigenes/submit   /my-guneku   /review
```

It declares `Host: https://www.guneku.org` and points at `/sitemap.xml`.

The member area and the moderation queues are excluded **twice** — here, and by
`robots: { index: false }` in each page's own metadata. Both are wanted and they do
different jobs: the meta tag is what a crawler that ignores robots.txt sees, and the
disallow stops the request being made at all. Verified on production: `/my-guneku`,
`/review/claims` and `/sign-in` all serve `noindex, nofollow`.

Neither is a security control. Those routes redirect a signed-out caller to sign-in and
every API behind them checks the session server-side — verified, every member endpoint
answers `401` unauthenticated.

### Sitemap

`/sitemap.xml`, generated by `src/app/sitemap.ts`. **121 URLs.** What may appear is decided
by `src/lib/visibility.ts` — the same predicate the internal search index uses — rather than
by filters written out again in the sitemap.

Present: the public core, the Palace and Fondom articles, quarters, institutions, projects,
updates, photo albums, and **the nine public businesses**. Absent: held records, private and
transactional routes, and anything whose content lives on another page.

### The individual Indigenes pages are deliberately not in the sitemap

There are **113** register entries at `/indigenes/founding/<slug>`. None of them is in the
sitemap, and that is existing, deliberate behaviour — not a defect, and **not touched by this
build**, on Marcel's instruction.

What each one is: a thin, unclaimed entry holding a name, an office, a source and little
else, published so the person can recognise it and claim it. The pages themselves are
`index, follow` and are reachable — from `/indigenes`, from the body rosters, from internal
search, and now from the business pages. They are simply not *submitted*.

**This is a decision worth Marcel making consciously, not a setting to flip.** See section C.

### Things the visual pass changed under this document's feet

Four principal pages were reachable from nowhere a reader could find, and are now in the
menu: `/explore`, `/quarters`, `/institutions` and `/sons-and-daughters`. All four were
already in the sitemap at 0.8 — the site was telling a search engine they mattered while
offering no way in. Internal linking is therefore materially better than when section B was
written, and the internal-linking item on the checklist is largely satisfied.

The homepage index grew from eight destinations to twelve and its heading changed from
"Explore Guneku" — which was the literal page title of `/explore` — to "Find your way around
Guneku".

Developer language was removed from five published pages. Nothing else in this document is
affected.

### Canonicals

Every page carries an absolute `rel="canonical"` on the `www` host. Spot-checked on
production across the home page, `/fondom`, a business page, a register entry and `/watch` —
all correct, all self-referencing, none pointing at a redirect.

The `guneku.vercel.app` preview host permanently redirects every path to the same path on
`www.guneku.org`, so a shared preview URL cannot compete for the same content.

### Metadata baseline

Titles run 25–60 characters and all end in the same `| Guneku Fondom` suffix. Descriptions
run 99–227.

Every page sampled carries `og:title`, `og:image` and `twitter:card`.

### Structured data

Site-wide: `Organization` (with `PostalAddress`) and `WebSite`.
Home: `FAQPage` with `Question`/`Answer`.
`/watch`: `VideoObject` and `CreativeWorkSeries`.

Nothing else is typed. See the checklist.

### Public business indexing

Nine public businesses are indexed and in the sitemap. **Vicky and Son's is held** — its
page 404s, it is absent from the sitemap, absent from internal search, and none of its
words is findable. That is correct and should stay that way until the Fondom names who runs
it.

### What is intentionally not published at all

Held archive material under `archive-held/` and `archive-staging/` — 181 files — is tracked
in git, sits outside `public/`, is absent from the build output and **404s on production**.
Verified. The 62 unreviewed channel uploads in `video-discovered.json` are likewise not
public and not indexed (R-043).

## C. Decisions for Marcel

Only genuine decisions. Everything implementable without an owner fact has been done.

### C1 · Should the 113 register entries be submitted for indexing?

This is the real question in this document.

**As they stand they are not submitted.** They are indexable and linked, just not offered.

*For submitting:* a son of Guneku searching his own name finds his entry and claims it, which
is the entire purpose of the register. That is a strong argument.

*Against:* 113 thin pages holding a name, an office and a source, all sharing a template, are
exactly the shape search engines treat as thin content, and they would become the largest
single class of page on the site. They may dilute rather than add.

*A middle path:* submit the entries that carry more than the minimum — an office, a body, a
linked business, a published profile — and leave the barest ones discoverable but not
submitted. Roughly 40 of the 113 carry a body or a notable standing.

No recommendation is made here because it trades reach against how the Fondom wants its
people represented, and that is the Palace's call rather than an engineering one.

### C2 · Do the published people want to be findable by name?

Related and prior to C1. The register publishes real people who have not asked to be
published, on the understanding that they may claim or remove their entry. Submitting them
to a search engine changes what that means in practice. Worth a Palace view before C1.

### C3 · A Google Business Profile for the Fondom or the Palace?

Would strengthen local signals. It also creates a public surface with reviews and
user-submitted photographs that somebody has to watch. Owner decision.

### C4 · The nine businesses in the directory

Their inclusion in the sitemap is already live and correct. The decision is whether the
Fondom wants to actively promote them — `LocalBusiness` structured data makes them eligible
for richer results, which is a service to them and also ties the Fondom's domain to their
commercial reputation.

### C5 · R-042, the image originals

An image-delivery decision the owner already deferred once. It belongs to this pass because
it affects Core Web Vitals.
